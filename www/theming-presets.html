Theming Three layers Tokens Opacity — the game behind the panel Skins — where a Theme cannot reach Your own preset — one file Readability is measured

A preset bundles all three. Fields you leave empty fall back to the defaults.

LayerDecidesHow you change it
Theme Colours, sizes, text scale, and the shape of everything the engine draws — button panels, inputs, tabs, toggle artwork Author a .tres, or generate one with tools/make_theme.py
GoSkin The shape of everything code draws — the joystick, quick-slot faces, the coach-mark ring, chips, skeletons, dividers, section headings Subclass it and override only the methods you care about
GoIconSet Icons by name — 84 default drawings, or the medieval set's 16 engraved drawings over them. A texture pack or an icon font; the calling code is identical either way Supply a set, or override just a few names
Why a Theme alone is not enough. Rounded is the only corner StyleBoxFlat has — set the radius to zero and you get a right angle; there is nothing in between. Diagonally cut corners, a single thickened edge, an outer glow: the engine draws none of them. And the joystick and coach mark are drawn in _draw(), where a theme cannot reach at all.

Picking one

GoUi.use_preset(GoThemePresets.SCIFI_DARK)

That one line swaps theme, skin and icons. From the editor, use Project Settings → Gohud → Theme → Preset; from a settings resource, GoConfig.preset.

Keeping one field your own

GoUi.use_preset(GoThemePresets.SCIFI_DARK)
GoUi.config.icons = my_icons     # your icons, the preset's everything else

Setting theme, skin or icons explicitly wins over the preset.

Medieval: iron, leather and parchment

medieval_dark pairs dark iron and leather with antique gold. medieval_light uses parchment and ink. Both use GoSkinMedieval, the existing widgets, and 16 original icons with engraving details; navigation icons fall back to the original set.

GoUi.use_preset(GoThemePresets.MEDIEVAL_DARK)
# Or: GoUi.use_preset(GoThemePresets.MEDIEVAL_LIGHT)

Choose the preset before creating your UI. Explicit GoConfig.theme, skin or icons overrides still take precedence. Open res://addons/gohud/examples/medieval/medieval.tscn and press F6 for a working character sheet, satchel and quest journal. Its buttons switch variants and open item details.

Menu frames have a rivet and small corner ornament; the always-visible HUD keeps a quiet edge. HP, mana and stamina use red, blue and olive fills. Cinzel is scoped to titles and subtitles; body text keeps the host/default font. The bundled font includes its license at assets/fonts/cinzel/OFL.txt.

The engraved set redraws bag, book, box, coin, crown, flag, heart, key, map, potion, shield, star, sword and user, and adds scroll and seal. Every other name falls back to the default set, so close and settings keep their default drawings in every preset. Cinzel covers Latin script; give localized headings a font of their own through shape.fonts.

What the suite checks for both variants. The medieval skin is selected; menu and HUD panels are forged frames, with the ornament on menus only; titles use Cinzel while body text keeps the inherited font; engraved icons import as DPITexture; a real quick slot uses the skin; changing a copied skin's dials reaches the slot without touching the preset; and switching back restores the default and sci-fi geometry.

Make a kingdom of your own

python3 addons/gohud/tools/new_theme.py kingdom --from medieval_dark
# Edit themes/palettes/kingdom.json, then:
python3 addons/gohud/tools/make_theme.py kingdom
godot --headless --path . --import
# In GDScript, before creating the widgets:
GoUi.use_preset(&"kingdom")

JSON themes can inherit other JSON themes. Omitted values keep their parent's settings; circular inheritance is rejected. The generated preset keeps its parent's icon set.

SettingEffect
shape.material0: iron · 1: leather · 2: parchment. Controls the surface marks.
shape.grain_alphaTexture opacity. Use 0 for a clean surface.
shape.ornament_scale · shape.bevel_strengthMenu corner ornament size and bevel highlight intensity.
shape.fontsMap title, subtitle, caption, body and button roles to addon-local res:// font files. Omitted roles keep their inherited font.
skin.dialsQuick-slot radius, rivet visibility, grain and highlight. The full table below lists each dial.

Material 3: a look for app screens New

material_light and material_dark bring Material 3 Expressive to gohud — the shapes, sizes and type of the M3 components, with every number read from the official M3 token set (material-web, tokens/versions/latest). Reach for them where a screen should feel like an app rather than a game menu: shops, inboxes, account and settings pages, leaderboards, a companion app.

GoUi.use_preset(GoThemePresets.MATERIAL_LIGHT)
# Or: GoUi.use_preset(GoThemePresets.MATERIAL_DARK)

What changes

PartIn Material 3
ButtonsPills (the full corner). The plate is 40dp tall, centred in the 48dp touch target, so the press area does not shrink; a compact button's plate is 32dp. Labels use Roboto at weight 500.
Dialogs and sheets28dp corners (extra-large), 24dp padding and a 24sp headline — GoDialogs, GoSurface, GoSheet and every popup built on them.
CardsFilled cards with 12dp corners and 16dp padding.
Fields and slidersOutlined text fields; the Expressive slider with its tall handle.
Chips and segments8dp corners (small).
App componentsGoNavBar, GoAppBar, GoFab, GoSearchBar, the chips, GoProgress and GoDatePicker follow the M3 component tokens.
TypeThe bundled Roboto for titles, subtitles, captions and buttons. Body text keeps your project's font, so Korean, Japanese, Chinese or Arabic still draw.
OpacityEvery panel is opaque — M3 surfaces are solid. (Default, sci-fi and medieval panels are 80% opaque, so the game shows through.)
SkinGoSkinMaterial: the drawer is the M3 modal side sheet, the console face is the M3 floating toolbar, banners sit on surface_container_low.
Where the spec gives way to legibility. M3 fades a disabled label to 38%; gohud keeps it at 4.5:1 so it can still be read. Snackbars keep a light plate, because their text colour is a token the widget picks — an inverse plate would put dark text on dark. tools/check_contrast.py measures both looks like every other.

Roboto is under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (assets/fonts/roboto/OFL.txt) and is used by the Material presets only.

Kids: the toy box New

kids_light (cream and peach with a grape primary) and kids_dark (blueberry ground, sunny-yellow primary, mint pills) are a look made for children's games and learning apps — chunky, bright and squishy, the kind of thing a small finger wants to press.

GoUi.use_preset(&"kids_light")
# Or: GoUi.use_preset(&"kids_dark")

What changes

PartIn the kids looks
Every faceA jelly candy, drawn by GoStyleBoxJelly: a chunky outline, a solid lip under everything you press, a body lighter on top and deeper below, and a white shine at the top left. Keys, list rows, folding titles, fields, bars, sliders and panels all wear it.
PressingThe lip goes and the face sinks into its place, then a jelly squish — wide and flat for a moment, then an elastic spring back. It runs on every button gohud makes; reduce_motion turns it off.
Fields, tabs, barsA text field is the same jelly turned in — a sunken well. Tabs are bubbles, bars have a jelly shine.
ColourEach kind of control in its own crayon colour, chosen by the same generator as every look, so every contrast gate still holds.
WindowsA sunny ribbon (mint at night) behind a window's title — the title shrinks, down to 15, to stay on one line — and a round candy close button in the danger crayon with a white cross.
Skin partsGoSkinKids: quick slots are candy blocks that sink while cooling down, badges are stickers, chips and the navigation pill are small jellies, avatars are round candies, the joystick is a rainbow, charts use crayons, alerts and notices are sticker notes with a solid rim.
OpacityPanels, cards, notices and popups are opaque.
No size changes. The outline and the lip are drawn inside each control's rectangle and every face keeps the padding the theme gave it, so a screen laid out in another look keeps its layout, and a host that pins its own sizes measures the same parts. Your project's font is kept too, so non-Latin scripts still draw.

Comic: bold ink New

comic_light (white panels on a paper-cream page, a cobalt primary) and comic_dark (slate panels on a night page, cream outlines, a yellow primary) draw every face like a comic-book panel — a bold ink outline with a hard shadow behind it.

GoUi.use_preset(&"comic_light")
# Or: GoUi.use_preset(&"comic_dark")

What changes

PartIn the comic looks
Every faceDrawn by GoStyleBoxComic: the surface colour inside a bold outline in the ink colour, and a hard shadow down and to the right. Keys, list rows, fields, tabs, bars, sliders, panels and popups all wear it.
PressingThe shadow goes and the face sinks into its place.
ControlsToggles, check boxes, radio buttons and slider handles are inked too, each with its own small shadow.
WindowsA yellow caption behind a window's title and an inked round close button.
Skin partsGoSkinComic: quick slots, chips, badges, avatars, notices, the navigation bar, the FAB and the date cells are inked; the joystick knob casts a shadow; charts use bold primaries.
OpacityPanels, cards, notices and popups are opaque; HUD cards are 92% opaque.

Ink and shadow settings

Three GoConfig settings change every comic face at once. Nothing is rebuilt — the screen redraws as you set them.

GoUi.config.comic_border_width = 2.0   # the outline (dp) — 3 by default
GoUi.config.comic_shadow_size = 6.0    # the shadow (dp) — 4 by default
GoUi.config.comic_shadow = false       # no shadows anywhere

One widget can go its own way, whatever the setting says:

GoStyle.comic_shadow(skip_button, false)     # this button sits flat
GoStyle.comic_shadow(card, true, true)       # this card and everything in it keep their shadow
GoStyle.comic_border(title, 5.0)             # a thicker outline on this one
No size changes. The outline and the shadow are drawn inside each control's rectangle and every face keeps the padding the theme gave it, so a screen laid out in another look keeps its layout. Your project's font is kept too.

Arcade: painted keys and thick boards New

arcade_light (white boards on a sky-blue page) and arcade_dark (navy boards on a night page) dress every screen like an arcade cabinet: every key is painted — a gradient from a light top to a deeper bottom, a white gloss stroke and a lip that sinks when pressed, inside a navy ink outline — and every board is thick, a coloured frame round a pale well.

GoUi.use_preset(&"arcade_light")
# Or: GoUi.use_preset(&"arcade_dark")

What changes

PartIn the arcade looks
KeysDrawn by GoStyleBoxArcade: a gradient body, a white gloss round the top-left corner, a deeper lip and a navy ink outline. The primary key is green, normal keys and the chosen tab blue, danger keys coral and red, small keys and the other tabs lavender.
LabelsWhite with an ink outline on every painted key. Every paint is moved until that label reads at every height of its gradient — 4.6:1 or more, outline counted as part of the letter, as WCAG does.
PressingThe lip goes, the key sinks into its place and pops back.
BoardsPanels, cards, HUD cards and popups are thick: a coloured frame (6dp round a window, 4 round a card or a HUD card, 3 round a menu or a notice) with its own gloss and lip, round a pale well with an ink line. Their padding grows a little to hold it, so text never touches the frame.
Fields and barsText fields and bar grooves are sunken wells; a bar's fill is a glossy painted tube in the bright fill of its colour.
WindowsA gold banner with a white, ink-outlined title, and a round red close key with a white cross.
Skin partsGoSkinArcade: quick slots and chips are key caps, a cooling slot lights a gold rim, badges are light plates, alerts are framed in their colour, the chosen card lights a gold rim, the FAB is a gold key and the joystick a glossy pad with four arrows.
OpacityPanels, cards, notices and popups are opaque; HUD cards are 94% opaque.

Paint one key its own colour

The theme paints keys by kind. A menu whose keys each want their own colour paints them one by one — the hover face comes a little lighter, the pressed one a little deeper, and the disabled face stays dim:

menu.add_child(GoStyle.arcade_paint(GoStyle.button("Resume", _resume, GoStyle.Tone.PRIMARY), Color("#4ACB3E")))
menu.add_child(GoStyle.arcade_paint(GoStyle.button("Shop", _shop), Color("#FF8A1E")))
GoStyle.arcade_paint(stage_card, Color("#E3343D"), true)   # a board's frame, and every key inside it
The label always reads. A paint the white label would not read on is moved lighter or deeper until it does, so the colour you pass is a starting point. Under the other looks arcade_paint() does nothing.
Keys keep their size. The lip and the gloss are drawn inside each key's rectangle, so a screen laid out in another look keeps its layout. Boards pad their content a little more (a card and a HUD card 12dp, 15 at the bottom) so the text never touches the frame. Your project's font is kept too.
